ADS401 Experimental Design

AI Integration at XJTLU

A proposed study of whether generative AI and responsible-use guidance improve students’ task performance, learning, and experience.

1. Why conduct a formative study?

Yes. Before the main experiment, we would conduct short interviews or a survey with 8–12 XJTLU students (and, if possible, several teachers). This identifies realistic uses of AI, such as explaining concepts, summarising reading, coding support and English writing, as well as concerns about inaccuracy, academic integrity and prompt-writing ability.

2. Participants and task

Participants: 40–60 XJTLU undergraduate or postgraduate students, recruited across disciplines. They should have experience of completing English-medium academic tasks.

Task: Read a short course-related English text and data table; write a concise summary, answer comprehension questions, and propose a simple evidence-based solution. The task is identical for all groups.

3–4. Independent variables and experimental design

IV1: AI availability
Generative AI available / not available
IV2: AI guidance
Responsible-use guidance provided / not provided

This is a 2 × 2 between-group factorial design. Each participant completes only one condition, reducing carryover and learning effects. There are four experimental conditions.

5. Dependent variables

OutcomeHow it is measured
Task performanceRubric-based score for accuracy, completeness and reasoning quality.
EfficiencyTime in minutes to complete the task.
LearningShort individual comprehension test completed without AI.
Cognitive workloadShort workload rating, for example a 1–7 scale or NASA-TLX items.
Trust and responsible useRatings of AI reliability plus evidence of checking sources and acknowledging AI use.

6. Baseline condition

The baseline is students’ normal non-AI study approach: they receive the same learning materials, task, device, time limit and scoring criteria, but cannot use generative AI. This makes AI availability and guidance the key differences between groups.

7 & 9. Procedure

1
Consent and background survey
Collect discipline, year of study and prior AI experience (about 10 minutes).
2
Random assignment
Allocate each student to one of the four conditions; show guidance only when required (about 5 minutes).
3
Academic task
Complete the standardised learning task (about 25 minutes).
4
Learning test and questionnaire
Complete a no-AI quiz and ratings of workload, trust and experience (about 20 minutes).

Total duration per participant: approximately 55–65 minutes.

Research focus

The study asks whether AI improves performance and efficiency, and whether guidance helps students use AI more critically and responsibly rather than simply relying on its output.
