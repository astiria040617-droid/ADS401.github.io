The AI we are evaluating

Meet Career Buddy Xiaoshang.

Career Buddy Xiaoshang (职业搭子小商) is the AI agent used in our study. Students can chat with it when they feel uncertain about career planning and ask for advice.

Our evaluation compares two ways of presenting career advice: information-focused advice and the same practical advice with emotional support.

01 / Research focus

The question behind the design.

Career decisions can involve both a need for practical information and a need for emotional support.

Does the effect of AI career advice style on users’ emotional responses and trust differ between users with Thinking–Feeling preference?

Target users: XJTLU students with career planning needs.

AI advice styleT/F preferenceEmotional responsesTrust
02 / Study design

Two styles. Two preference groups.

AI advice style is assigned; T/F decision-making preference is measured before the study.

MANIPULATED CONDITION

AI advice style

A: information-focused advice. B: the same practical advice with added emotional support.

MEASURED GROUPING VARIABLE

T/F preference

Thinking (T) or Feeling (F) decision-making preference, measured by a pre-study questionnaire.

PRIMARY OUTCOMES

Emotional responses & trust

Compare participants’ reported emotional responses and trust after reading the assigned AI response.

SECONDARY MEASURE

Perceived empathy

PETS can check whether participants perceive Condition B as more empathic.

03 / Experiment plan

From recruitment to response.

Planned recruitment: 20–40 university students through convenience sampling, with an effort to recruit participants from both T and F groups.

01

Pre-study questionnaire

Measure each participant’s T/F preference.

02

Random assignment

Within each preference group, randomly assign participants to one of the two AI advice conditions.

03

Collect responses

Record the assigned condition and T/F grouping, collect questionnaire responses and brief interview comments.

Rationale. Prior chatbot experiments have compared informational with emotional support by assigning participants to different response conditions. This design explores whether the effect differs across users with T/F preferences.
