The AI we are evaluating

Meet Career Buddy Xiaoshang.

Career Buddy Xiaoshang (职业搭子小商) is the AI agent used in our study. Students can chat with it when they feel uncertain about career planning and ask for advice.

Our evaluation compares two AI response styles: analytical advice, which focuses on practical information and reasoning, and empathetic advice, which acknowledges users’ feelings while offering career guidance.

01 / Research focus

The question behind the design.

Career decisions can involve both a need for practical information and a need for emotional support.

Does the effect of AI career advice style on users’ emotional responses and trust differ between users with Thinking–Feeling preference?

Target users: XJTLU students with career planning needs.

AI advice styleT/F preferenceEmotional responsesTrust
02 / Study design

Two styles. Two preference groups.

A 2 × 2 mixed factorial design: T/F preference is a measured between-subjects factor, and AI advice style is a within-subjects factor. Every participant evaluates both A and B.

MANIPULATED CONDITION

AI advice style

A: analytical advice focused on practical information and reasoning. B: empathetic advice that acknowledges users’ feelings while offering career guidance.

MEASURED GROUPING VARIABLE

T/F preference

Thinking (T) or Feeling (F) decision-making preference, based on the MBTI Thinking–Feeling framework and explored with our pre-study questionnaire. This questionnaire is not the official MBTI assessment.

PRIMARY OUTCOMES

Emotional responses & trust

Compare each participant’s reported emotional responses and trust after A and after B, then examine whether the difference varies by T/F preference.

SECONDARY MEASURE

Perceived empathy

PETS can check whether participants perceive the empathetic advice as more empathic.

Participant grouping & survey structure.

Explore participants' MBTI-inspired decision preferences and initial expectations.

Survey Structure & Grouping

02 · Decision PreferencesA group-designed T/F scale (1-5 points) based on the MBTI framework. Used for Median Split grouping.

04 · Experiment InvitationCollects willingness to participate in the formal experiment and contact information.

*Grouping Method: We collected ~80 responses initially. After excluding non-target groups, 42 valid samples remained. Participants were divided into T and F groups using a Median Split on the mean score of the five T/F items in Part 2 (Q5–Q9) of the pre-study questionnaire, with a cut-off of 3.4. Scores ≥ 3.4 were classified as T and scores < 3.4 as F, so no Undetermined group was created.

04 / Experiment plan

From recruitment to response.

Planned recruitment: 42 valid XJTLU students through convenience sampling. To avoid uneven groups, T/F grouping was determined using a Median Split on the mean score of the five Part 2 T/F items in the pre-study questionnaire (cut-off = 3.4).

Design2 × 2 mixed factorial; every participant evaluates both AI styles.
SamplingConvenience sampling of 42 XJTLU students; recruitment is not random.
CounterbalancingThe system randomly assigns the order of styles (AB or BA) to control for order effects.
Data collectionPre-study questionnaire, GEW, TIAS, PETS, and brief interview comments.

Complete Experimental Procedure

Step Content
Step 0 Participants read and sign the informed consent form.
Step 1: Scenario & Baseline Participants read a standard interview failure scenario to induce career anxiety. After reading, they immediately complete GEW 1 as baseline emotional data.
Step 2: Interaction 1 Participants input the first preset script (expressing anxiety about the interview failure). Based on random assignment, the system presents the first AI response style (Analytical or Empathetic). After reading, they complete GEW 2/3 to measure emotional changes.
Step 3: Interaction 2 Participants input the second preset script (expressing future job-hunting plans). The system presents the second AI response style (different from Step 2). After reading, they complete GEW 2/3 to measure emotional changes.
Step 4: Comprehensive Evaluation After experiencing both styles, participants complete a post-study questionnaire. They evaluate their trust (TIAS) and perceived empathy (PETS) separately for the "first style" and the "second style".
Step 5: Interview A brief semi-structured interview is conducted to ask about participants' subjective preferences and reasons. Finally, we express our gratitude (Debriefing).
05 / Experimental materials

Scripts and AI responses.

Wizard of Oz control: Pre-written text responses delivered via a simulated chat interface.

STEP 1 · SCENARIO

"Imagine you are about to graduate and job hunt. Today you just had an interview at a very desirable company. But you feel you performed terribly and your answers were logically messy. Now, you feel anxious and confused, so you open Career Buddy for help."

INTERACTION SCRIPTS

Script 1: "I feel like I completely messed up the interview today, my logic was all over the place. I'm so anxious, what should I do?"

Script 2: "What should I do next? Should I keep sending resumes to other companies? It feels hopeless."

Analytical Style (A1)

"Based on your interview failure, we need a structured review... 1. Use the STAR method. 2. Build a question bank. Data shows 3 mock interviews significantly improve pass rates. Do you need me to generate a template?"

Empathetic Style (B1)

"I completely understand your frustration. It's normal to be nervous in front of a dream company... You don't need to blame yourself. Take a deep breath. Which part made you feel the worst?"

06 / Evaluation methods

What the study plans to collect.

Detailed measurement instruments and scoring criteria.

Primary Measure · Emotion

Geneva Emotion Wheel (GEW 3.0)

Administered 3 times: Baseline (GEW 1), after Interaction 1 (GEW 2), and after Interaction 2 (GEW 3). Participants rate the intensity of their emotional response on a 5-point scale (1 = weakly, 5 = strongly) across 20 emotion families, or select "No emotion".

Interest, Pride, Relief, Regret, Contempt
Amusement, Joy, Compassion, Shame, Hate
Pleasure, Contentment, Sadness, Disappointment, Anger
Admiration, Love, Guilt, Fear, Disgust

*Note: Researchers must match GEW 2 to the Empathetic style and GEW 3 to the Analytical style based on the randomized presentation order.

Primary Measure · Trust

Trust in Automation Scale (TIAS)

Administered at Step 4. Evaluated for both styles. 12 items, 7-point Likert scale (1 = Not at all, 7 = Extremely). Items 1-5 are reverse-scored. "The system" is replaced by "The AI assistant".

Items (1-5 Reverse Scored):
1. The system is deceptive.
2. The system behaves in an underhanded manner.
3. I am suspicious of the system's intent, action, or output.
4. I am wary of the system.
5. The system's action will have a harmful outcome.
Items (6-12):
6. I am confident in the system.
7. The system provides security.
8. The system has integrity.
9. The system is dependable.
10. The system is reliable.
11. I can trust the system.
12. I am familiar with the system.
Secondary Measure · Empathy

Perceived Empathy of Technology Scale (PETS)

Administered at Step 4. Original scale uses a 101-point slider (strongly disagree to strongly agree). Contains 10 items across two factors. Note: Cannot be modified.

Emotional Response (PETS-ER, 6 items):
E1. The system considered my mental state.
E2. The system seemed emotionally intelligent.
E3. The system expressed emotions.
E4. The system sympathized with me.
E5. The system showed interest in me.
E6. The system supported me in coping with an emotional situation.
Understanding & Trust (PETS-UT, 4 items):
U1. The system understood my goals.
U2. The system understood my needs.
U3. I trusted the system.
U4. The system understood my intentions.

All five participants reported no Relief at baseline, but all reported Relief after both reply styles. Two of the three T participants reported 2 points more Relief after the analytical style than after the empathetic style; the third reported the same score for both. The two F participants gave identical Relief scores under both styles — suggesting some T-type participants draw Relief from actionable advice. The group difference was not significant (p = .219). For the F group, mean Sadness dropped from 2.5 after the empathetic style to 0.5 after the analytical style.

Current conclusion. The advantage of the empathetic style is mainly in perceived emotional response; the advantage of the analytical style is in understanding users’ needs. For some participants, concrete advice also comes with a stronger sense of Relief. The two groups did respond differently — the T group’s direction matches the prediction, while the F group was roughly balanced between the two styles. All interaction tests are non-significant, and with N = 5 these results are exploratory only.
07 / Execution checklist & script

Researcher on-site checklist.

Includes preparation steps and exact scripts for the experimenter.

Researcher Script & Checklist

Preparation Checklist:
  • [ ] Confirm informed consent is signed.
  • [ ] Confirm participant's T/F grouping (check pre-study list).
  • [ ] Confirm randomized presentation order and prepare the correct GEW form (GEW 2 for Empathetic, GEW 3 for Analytical).
  • [ ] Ensure audio recording device is ready for the interview.

Step 0: Consent Form
Participants read and sign the informed consent form.

Step 1: Scenario & Baseline
"Hello, thank you for participating! First, please read this job-seeking scenario carefully and try to immerse yourself in it. After reading, please complete GEW 1 based on your true feelings right now."

"Imagine you are about to graduate and job hunt. Today you just had an interview at a very desirable company. But you feel you performed terribly and your answers were logically messy. Now, you feel anxious and confused, so you open Career Buddy for help."

Step 2: First Interaction
"Next, you will have a conversation with the AI assistant. Please input this text first: 'I feel like I completely messed up the interview today, my logic was all over the place. I'm so anxious, what should I do?' Then read the AI's reply carefully, and fill out GEW 2/3 when you finish. (Reminder: Don't mix them up)."

Step 3: Second Interaction
"Now please continue to input the second text: 'What should I do next? Should I keep sending resumes to other companies? It feels hopeless.' Then read the AI's reply carefully, and fill out GEW 2/3 when you finish."

Step 4: Post-study Comprehensive Evaluation
"Thank you for the experience. Now, please comprehensively review the two AI replies just now. Please evaluate your trust (TIAS) and perceived empathy (PETS) separately for the 'first style' and the 'second style'."

Step 5: Interview
"Thank you for participating. Next, I will ask you three short questions to hear your true feelings about the two AI replies. The interview content is only for academic research, there are no right or wrong answers, and you can speak freely."
1. Which AI reply style do you personally prefer? Why?
2. If you really failed an interview in reality, which reply would make you feel better, or make you think it is more reliable?
3. If you were to use this AI career assistant in the future, what style would you want it to have? Is it one that gives you direct advice, or one that takes care of your emotions first?
